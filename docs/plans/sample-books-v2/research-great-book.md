**Pattern:** every competitor book is a stack of answered prompts (question as title → prose → photo at end), either verbatim-and-flat (StoryWorth) or AI-rewritten-and-fake (Remento). Nobody edits like a book editor; nobody treats one story as a two-page spread worth designing; nobody puts the family in the book.

## B. What great oral-history books do (rules + why)

1. **Cut the interviewer, keep the monologue.** Terkel's *Working* and StoryCorps' *Listening Is an Act of Love* print the speaker alone; Columbia's archive once "eliminated questions" as standard practice. The reader meets the person, not the process. ([CCOHR style guide](https://www.nypap.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/CCOHRTranscriptStyleGuide2018.pdf))
2. **Distil, don't rewrite.** StoryCorps: "edited from transcripts of forty-minute interviews… without altering the tone or meaning." Terkel: "a prospector for gold" — he cut and reordered but kept the ore. ([kitchensisters.org](https://kitchensisters.org/present/the-working-tapes-of-studs-terkel/), [penguinrandomhouse reader's guide](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/297812/listening-is-an-act-of-love-by-edited-with-an-introduction-by-dave-isay/readers-guide/))
3. **"Few subjects are eloquent unedited"** — remove repetition and false starts, never grammar or dialect. "Correcting grammar can erase dialect, rhythm, and identity." ([thenation.com](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/studss-people/), [gotranscript](https://gotranscript.com/en/blog/editing-oral-history-transcripts-what-to-fix-and-what-to-leave-examples))
4. **Spoken ≠ printed.** StoryCorps lets print and audio wording differ "slightly"; Portelli: the transcript loses pauses and tempo that carry meaning, so the recording stays the real document. Pair every printed story with its audio. ([scribd Portelli](https://www.scribd.com/document/135566347/portelli))
5. **A headnote, not a question.** Terkel opens each piece with a short italic note (who, where, the room, the hands); StoryCorps heads each story "NAME, age, interviews his/her mother NAME, age" and closes "Recorded in [city], [date]." (from the books themselves). Context in 20 words, then silence.
6. **Short pieces.** StoryCorps stories run 2–5 pages from 40 minutes of tape; HONY captions ~100 words from 1–2-hour interviews. One memory = one spread. ([guilfordian.com](https://www.guilfordian.com/news/2018/04/13/stanton-discusses-hony-roots-storytelling/))
7. **Stories must "explode off the page."** Isay picked 50 of 10,000 by whether they left a mark; not every answer belongs. Selection is the editing.
8. **Thematic over chronological.** *Listening* uses five themes (Home and Family, Work and Dedication, Journeys…); memoir editors warn a strict timeline "reads like a resume." Group by what the life was about; let dates sit in headnotes.
9. **Concrete particulars beat summary.** Alexievich hunts "surprising, concrete particulars" (a woman remembers white lilacs, not the battle); eulogy guides: "one small detail can be worth a thousand words." ([prospectmagazine](https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/magazine/interview-svetlana-alexievich-a-melody-of-voices), [Marie Curie eulogy guide](https://www.mariecurie.org.uk/globalassets/media/documents/how-we-can-help/information-for-bereaved-family-and-friends/arranging-the-ceremony/how-to-write-a-eulogy.pdf))
10. **No author commentary.** Alexievich: "My commentary would go out of date very quickly"; Stanton "intentionally removed himself." The editor is invisible.
11. **Let voices disagree.** Alexievich places opposing voices side by side "without resolving them"; eulogy guides: "one person may remember humor, another quiet kindness… weaving in several makes the person feel complete."
12. **Rhythm is the voice.** "It's a lot about the intonation, the tempo." Keep sentence length as spoken; keep the "I just", the "you know what I mean?" when it carries tone.
13. **Open with the strongest ordinary line, end on the speaker's last words,** not an editor's bow. (StoryCorps practice.)
14. **Interviewee approval.** Alexievich sends edits back; a Paris Review interviewee thanked her "for not letting me deface my own voice." The parent must recognise herself.
15. **How many memories make a life:** a personal-historian memoir ≈ 30,000 words / 115 pages; StoryWorth's typical ≈ 50 stories / 250 pp; StoryCorps' *Listening* = 49 stories. 30–60 well-chosen stories, not 52 dutiful ones. ([picturesandstories](https://picturesandstories.squarespace.com/how-and-how-much))

## C. What readers find fake

- **AI "tidying"**: "It paraphrases. It 'tidies.' It rewrites" — the top Remento complaint.
- **Lessons and neat resolutions**: AI drafts "pull toward metaphors, lessons, and neat resolutions… If the lesson isn't visible, it will construct one and write toward it." ([carolinecala substack](https://carolinecala.substack.com/p/may-the-process-be-with-you/comments))
- **Shiny, generic prose**: "well-polished, shiny, and clean" but "indistinct, nondescript"; "Bag of chips. No dips. No salsa." ([brookewarner.substack.com](https://brookewarner.substack.com/p/why-does-it-matter-that-im-reading))
- **Emotional summary instead of scene** ("she carried that with her forever").
- **Wikipedia's tells**: "a testament to", "pivotal", "tapestry", "not just X but Y", rule-of-three lists, trailing "-ing" clauses ("reflecting a lifetime of…"), every paragraph ending on a wrap-up sentence, "Challenges and Legacy"-style sections. ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing))
- **Corporate scaffolding**: summary boxes, stat blocks, labelled layers, "Key takeaways" — product UI leaking onto paper.
- **Printed prompts as titles** ("Prompts were automatically inserted… I really disliked this").
- **Dead links**: QR codes that depend on a startup's server.
- What moves instead: the ordinary voicemail ("the grocery-list version of your dad… who he actually was on a Tuesday"), the handwriting, the laugh, "stories we never knew or thought to ask", contradictions, the one-line answer that is exactly how she talks. ([propublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/kiss-everybody-parents-voicemails-preserve-their-memory-in-death), [deaddadspodcast](https://agents.deaddadspodcast.com/dad-s-last-voicemail-his-voice-is-still-in-your-phone-now-what))

## D. Print conventions checklist (keepsake hardcover)

- **Trim**: 6×9 (StoryWorth) reads as "a real book"; 7×10 / 8×10 lets photos breathe. Pick one; 7×10 is the compromise.
- **Front matter, in order, roman folios, no running heads**: half-title (recto) → blank/frontispiece photo (verso) → title page (recto) → copyright/colophon (verso) → dedication (recto) → epigraph (optional; best: one line in her words) → contents (recto). ([thebookdesigner.com](https://thebookdesigner.com/parts-of-a-book/))
- **Body**: part/chapter openers on a recto with a sink (title ⅓ down), no running head, drop folio; 2–3-line drop cap on first paragraph only; every story opener may be a spread.
- **Running heads**: verso = her name, recto = chapter/theme title, small caps; folio on outer margin; none on openers, blanks, photo pages. ([hadleyhouse](https://hadleyhouse.substack.com/p/designing-book-running-heads))
- **Pull quotes**: one per spread max, her words only, set larger in the same face.
- **Photos**: caption under or beside every photo, small, roman or italic, consistent; names + year, who is who left-to-right; never "a treasured moment". ([friesens yearbookology](https://yearbookology.friesens.com/docs/chapter-4-placing-copy-captions-in-a-layout))
- **Paper**: 100–120 gsm uncoated, acid-free (StoryWorth's 70# "felt cheap"); matte for text, coated only if photo-first.
- **Back matter order**: afterword/letters → family tree or "the people in this book" → index of names → acknowledgements → colophon last. ([americanancestors](https://www.americanancestors.org/sites/default/files/2024-12/Class%205%20Publishing%20Process%20handout.pdf))
- **Colophon says**: typeface and its designer, paper, printer, how recorded ("from 14 phone conversations, March–June 2026"), edition number, who edited. Two sentences to half a page. ([diggypod](https://www.diggypod.com/blog/colophon))
- **Blank pages** at the end "for future generations to add".

## E. The 10x list (ranked; all paper, no app)

1. **Edit like Terkel, not transcribe like StoryWorth or rewrite like Remento**: her exact words, false starts removed, grammar and dialect untouched, 40 minutes cut to the 600 words that explode.
2. **Headnotes, not prompts**: each story opens with a 15–25-word italic line of scene ("She is at the kitchen table in Garden Grove; the rice cooker clicks off twice during this"), the question gone.
3. **One memory = one spread**, designed, with white space; no story crosses more than two spreads.
4. **Themes, not weeks**: 5–7 parts named in her words ("The Boat", "Sewing for Mrs. Lam"), not "Childhood / Career".
5. **The family answers on the facing page**: a daughter's 80-word memory of the same day, set in a second face, signed with a first name — the thing no competitor's book has.
6. **Short-answers pages**: a spread of 12 one-liners in her exact phrasing ("Favourite smell? Fish sauce and rain.") — readers say these are "exactly how she talks".
7. **The voice, offline-proof**: a small QR per story *and* a printed recording ID + the archive location in the colophon so the audio outlives any server.
8. **Her epigraph, her dedication**: both in her own spoken words, pulled from the tapes, not written for her.
9. **The people in this book**: a two-page who's-who with a one-line description in her words for each person, instead of a genealogy chart.
10. **A letter, not a lesson**: close with the one unprompted thing she said to the family ("if you're reading this, call your brother"), verbatim, and nothing after it but the colophon.
11. **Honest photos**: fewer, larger, captioned with names and years in her phrasing ("That's me pretending I liked him").
12. **A colophon that tells the truth about the making**: dates, number of calls, who edited, the rule "nothing was added that she did not say."

## F. Verdict on the founder's instinct

The instinct holds. Readers want the person; every device that reminds them a product made this costs trust. Keep what a good publisher would keep; cut what a dashboard would add.

| Device | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Printed question above each story | **Cut** (replace with headnote) | Top StoryWorth layout complaint; Terkel/StoryCorps never do it. A title in her words + 20-word headnote carries context. |
| "Asked by <family member>" line | **Keep, small, at the end** | It is the family's fingerprint on the book, and readers love "stories we never thought to ask"; set it like a StoryCorps "Recorded…" line, one point smaller, after the story. |
| Summary/abstract line above a story | **Cut** | Remento's 12-pt summaries are the thing that makes it read like a report; a story needs no abstract. |
| "We asked / going deeper" follow-up Q&A | **Cut** (fold the follow-up into the monologue) | Shows the machinery; Columbia-style Q&A is archive practice, not book practice. |
| "The lesson she carries" box | **Cut** | The number-one AI tell: "if the lesson isn't visible, it will construct one." Let her say it if she said it. |
| "Still alive / for Ellie" boxes | **Cut as boxes; keep as a closing letter** | A box is UI; a letter in her voice to a named grandchild is the most-read page in any such book. |
| Layer bars | **Cut** | Pure product scaffolding; meaningless on paper. |
| Stats pages | **Cut** | "14 calls, 3h12m" belongs in the colophon in one sentence, not a page. |
| "How this book was made" page | **Keep, one page, at the back** | Readers do want provenance and the "nothing added" promise; make it the colophon + afterword, not a feature page. |
| Family tree page | **Keep as "The people in this book"** | Needed for a stranger-grandchild in 2060; do it as names + her one-liners, not a chart. |
| "What she passes on" list | **Cut unless verbatim** | If it is a list she actually spoke, print it as a short-answers page; if assembled, it is a lesson box by another name. |
| QR codes to hear the voice | **Keep, small, one per story + archive ID** | The single most-praised feature in Remento reviews; the single most-feared failure (dead servers). Print an ID so it survives. |
| Family members' differing versions beside a story | **Keep** | Alexievich's polyphony; eulogy guidance; no competitor has it. Facing page, different face, first name only. |
| Short one-line answers pages | **Keep** | HONY-length truth; exactly how she talks; breaks up long stories. |
| Letters to the family | **Keep, one, last** | Only if unprompted or lightly prompted, verbatim. |
| Full transcripts | **Cut from the book** | Portelli: the transcript is not the document, the recording is. Offer transcripts as a separate archive PDF; the book is the edit. |
