## Editor's note (≤ 100 words)

It's a better book with the same score. Nearly all of round 2 is fixed, and the father and Grand Haven memories add the family life readers wanted. Four things keep it under 8.0:
- The climax never says who got the keys, and the letter's "ready in three" can't be decoded.
- Three photographs contradict their captions.
- m12 can't happen as written: a morning walk, home by eleven, a second-shift lead, and an evening headnote.
- The rendered pages still carry eight highlights, six QR cards and a numbered "Chapter Seven" quote wall.

Fix the first three in the data and the book should clear 7.8. Getting to 8.0 also needs the fourth.

**New and wrong:**
- **Keys unanswered (m5 and the letter).**
  - m5 is titled "Who gets the keys". It ends with "It was Elena's idea and Tomás did the work. Fine. Both." Then Tomás says "After the sale I opened my own shop."
  - Nobody says who runs the company now.
  - The letter's "Tell your father he was ready in three" fits neither Tomás's "Five years, and I counted" nor his "I'd have taken the keys in 2016."
- **Photo vs caption: m3.** The caption reads "Burton Street, 1979. Marisol in the doorway, supervising." Marisol was born in 1977, so she'd be two. The child on the step looks five or six.
- **Photo vs caption: m13.** The caption reads "Grand Haven, 1987." The picture shows an inland lake with a wooded far shore, a swimming dock and moored boats. Grand Haven faces open Lake Michigan.
- **Photo vs caption: m8.** The caption reads "Hector's wagon, out front before anybody." A man stands at the wagon's open door pointing at the building, so somebody is already there. The wagon is also parked across the street, not out front. The building is a one-storey brick box with curtained windows on a street of houses, and it reads as an office, not a twelve-man shop.
- **Timeline: m12.** "I walk the floor before anybody's read their email" and "I'm home by eleven" clash with the headnote "on a Thursday evening, just back from the shop." They also clash with Maya's "The second-shift lead had a list on a clipboard… she waited until he'd done his walk."
- **Wage: m2.** "hiring tool and die men at twice that" (twice $3.25 is $6.50) is followed by "four dollars and ten cents," and nobody remarks on the gap.
- **Order: chapter 5.** m14 (June 1985) sits between m8's "January 1991" call and m9's "two weeks after the call from the lakeshore." The cliffhanger breaks, and the reader has to go back two memories to find the call.

**Facts that pass:**
- Feb 8, 1991 and Mar 25, 2022 were both Fridays.
- Every dateline age is right for a March 9, 1951 birthday.
- The money adds up: $11,000 − $2,300 − $540 − deposit and tooling ≈ "seven thousand and change".
- These counts check out:
  - Hector, 1984 + 38 = 2022.
  - "thirty-six years" of floor-walking, 1983–2019.
  - Five years of "not yet", 2014–2019.
  - "Fifty years" of marriage, Aug 1975 to 2025.
  - The Bridgeport, "forty-two years", 1983–2025.
- The m8 lost work was 60% × ½ = 30%, which matches "a third" in m9.
- 14 − 2 = 12 stayed.
- Front matter says "13 memories, 4 photographs", which is correct.
- Elena's "He's seventy-five" is true on the edition date, March 9, 2026, his 75th birthday.
- Child-safe: yes.

## Below the line (minor, for the fixer)

- **m5 / letter.**
  - After "Fine. Both." add one line saying who holds the keys now.
  - Change the letter's "Tell your father he was ready in three." to something Tomás's own count supports, for example "Tell your father he was ready in 2016. He counted right."
- **m3 photo.** Change "Burton Street, 1979. Marisol in the doorway, supervising." to 1982, or use a photo with a toddler.
- **m13 photo.** For "the same cottage at Grand Haven" and the caption "Grand Haven, 1987.", either swap the picture for a Lake Michigan beach or move the cottage to "Spring Lake, by Grand Haven", which is the inland lake the picture actually shows.
- **m8 photo.** Change "Hector's wagon, out front before anybody." to a caption that allows for the man at the door, for example "Clay Avenue, 1988, the day we went to look at it."
- **m12.**
  - Change the headnote "on a Thursday evening, just back from the shop" to "on a Thursday, just back from the shop."
  - In Maya's note, "The second-shift lead" should be "The first-shift lead", or he should go in for the afternoon.
- **m2.** Change "at twice that" to "at a dollar more", or make the hire "six dollars and a half".
- **m2 headnote.** "he gets up once to find a pay stub he has kept since 1968." Either show the stub in the text ("A dollar sixty an hour. I still have the stub.") or cut it from the headnote.
- **m4 plate.** The row "Dec · Payday · Fri" falls before anyone was hired ("By spring of ’84 I had more work than hands," m7). Label it as his own draw or move it to a 1984 page.
- **m6.** In "Marisol drew on the floor in chalk", Marisol is six on a weekday noon in "that first winter", so she'd be in first grade. Make it "On Saturdays Marisol drew…".
- **m9.** "Nine of the twelve were still there in 2022" means nine of twelve still working 31 years later. Use "Five of the twelve" or "retired from there".
- **m9 headnote.** "Alone in the shop after second shift; he stops for a long while before the part about the pallet." This has a retired man alone at midnight in a shop he sold, and the company reviewing its own tape. Use a place and nothing else.
- **m7 headnote.** In "he runs his thumb along the table to show what Hector did", say who is watching ("for Maya") or cut the gesture.
- **m11.**
  - "Every one of them still here." contradicts Tomás's "He sent Hector" (m5): Hector left at the sale.
  - "I signed the checks one more time, by hand" means 140 paper checks in 2022. Use "signed the payroll register", or keep it and accept the stretch.
- **m11.** "I didn't leave it on the table. I left it in a hundred and forty houses." is a press quote. Cut the second sentence or both.
- **m14.** "wiping down a pump handle somebody had left greasy" is the third wipe callback, after m1's fender and m14's bench. Keep the gas station and drop the pump handle.
- **letter.** "When you're seventy-five and somebody asks what you built, I hope you have trouble answering, because it's too many people to name." is a keynote closer. The letter is stronger ending at "eight o'clock" and then "Payday is Friday by noon."
- **Marisol's family page.** "Write down what's true, the night it's true." is a slogan. End on "She let me check the rest. I'm still checking."
- **people.** Tomás's line "Taught me to swim at seven." reads as Ray at seven. Use "Taught me to swim when he was seven."
- **little.**
  - "My father's six-inch dial caliper. It's off by a thousandth. I know which way." repeats m1 nearly word for word.
  - "My mother asking if I was cold." is the third telling, after m2 and the people page.
  - Replace one of each.
- **learned.** "from 'Elena's ledger' · 1983" dates a line he said in 2025 about 2004 (same class as Tom's 1968 flag).
- **Contents / opener.** "07 What Raymond learned" and "Chapter Seven" should be unnumbered like "Little questions" and "From the family".
- **Bylines.** Side notes read "ELENA ORTIZ, HIS WIFE" while family pages read "Remembered by Elena Ortiz, his wife". Marisol's and Maya's pages are reflections, not memories. Pick one byline style.
