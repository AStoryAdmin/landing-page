## Reader 1: Linda, 52, nurse in Ohio. Mother is 81 with early memory loss.

- (a) 9 — Now I know him off the clock. He stands in the lake "up to my knees and call[s] it swimming". He cries "at a gas station in Texarkana." He's "home for dinner" and missed the science fair. That's a man, not a company.
- (b) 8 — "He walked around it a long time and didn't say anything" is a real son watching a real father. "People can take bad news. What they can't take is finding out last" is a man reading his own poster.
- (c) 7 — My mother never ran anything, and half this book is payroll. But "My father comes north" and Elena's "He's seventy-five. He can read it here." are the two pages I'd want from her. I'd want the voice too, before it goes.
- (d) 8 — The cover finally reads as a place, not a lifespan. One sentence is highlighted for me ("no first name. Good."), and it's the one I'd have underlined myself. I'd keep the recordings.
- (e) Best: Elena's "The kitchen table", for "I never told him how close it was." Cut: page two of "Thursdays". Maya tells the same scrap-one, make-the-second mill story herself, and better ("Forty minutes of hm"), eight pages later.

## Reader 2: Marcus, 44, software manager. Father "doesn't talk about himself."

- (a) 9 — Broom, lathe, Falcon, credit union, garage, Hector, Clay Avenue, the pallet, the drawer, the ESOP, Thursdays. I could draw it, and now there's a lake in it.

## Editor's note (≤ 100 words)

The round-4 fixes landed. The book reads true, the father page now sits where its dates belong, and the m11 ending is his. It sits exactly on 8.0 with no margin. Three things would give it margin:
- Fix the m5 drawer/desk contradiction.
- Make the ledger plate match Elena's arithmetic.
- Put chapters 3 and 4 in an order where the garage exists before the reader is shown it.

Separately, two of these four photographs appear on the homepage as another family's (see "New and wrong"). That is the single fastest way for a visitor to stop believing the sample.

## Listen cards and the margin highlight (noted separately; scores unchanged)

The four memory-page listen cards (m4, m14, m9, m11, "Scan with a phone camera to hear Raymond tell this") and the one margin highlight (m14, "no first name. Good.") are real page design and will stay. Here is what they cost in the scores above:

- **Cost:** together they cost about 3 points of 25 on (d). That is −0.6 on the (d) mean (7.4 against about 8.0) and −0.15 on the book mean (8.00 against about 8.15).
- **Split:** the cards cost about −0.3 on (d) (Priya, plus half of Marcus) and the highlight about −0.3 (Dave, plus half of Marcus). On its own, removing only the cards gains +0.2 on (d) (Priya). Removing only the highlight also gains +0.2 (Dave). Marcus moves only when both go.
- **Cheapest trade if the owner wants margin:** keep the cards and move the highlight off m14 to a page nobody grieves on (m2's "show you twice" or m4). Dave's 7 is about *where* it is, not that it exists.

**New and wrong:**
- **Same photographs, different family, on the homepage.** This is site-level, not scored, and the most important item here.
  - `/mission/03` is Raymond's "Burton Street, 1982. Marisol on the step". On the homepage it is "The house on Maple Drive · 1973" (`src/lib/homeExamples.ts:69–71`, shown by `WhatGetsLost`). `Guide.tsx:52` also calls it "around 1973".
  - `/mission/30` is Raymond's Spring Lake, 1987. It also rises at year 2005 on the homepage intro timeline (`src/pages/site/home/introGather.ts:67`).
  - (`OpenBook.tsx` captions it "The lake, the year Dan learned to swim", but that component isn't imported anywhere, so it isn't live.)
  - A visitor who goes from home to /samples sees one family's snapshot dated nine years apart under two families' names. That breaks (b) for every reader at once.
- **m5 drawer vs desk (same spread).**
  - Ray (p.32): "I kept it in a drawer three months and didn't tell Tomás."
  - Elena (p.33): "I cut it out and put it on his desk, under the offer, and I left it there a month."
  - The offer can't be in the drawer and on the desk. Fix: "I put it in the drawer, under the offer" and "I moved it to the top every Monday".
- **Ledger plate vs Elena's arithmetic.**
  - Elena's page says the first page holds the mill, "the deposit, tooling and three months' rent". The plate shows Mill, one Rent line, First order, Kerosene, Bracket job and Our draw. There is no deposit, no tooling, and one month of rent.
  - The plate mixes income (412, 1,150) and spending (2,300, 180, 38) in one unsigned column. For "the best bookkeeper in Kent County" that is the one page a Marisol-type reader will check.
  - Fix: add "Sep · Tooling" and "Aug · Deposit" rows and make the rent "Aug–Oct · Rent ×3 · 540". Or give the plate a second money column (in / out).
  - Also, "I never told him how close it was" doesn't land, because $7,000 sounds comfortable. One clause on the monthly burn would make it land ("that was five months if nobody paid us").
- **Chapter 3 / 4 order.** Moving m14 fixed chapter 5 but broke chapter 3:
  - m4 "Elena's ledger" (1983–2004) shows the reader the 1991 near-failure, the partner papers and the first ledger page before chapter 4 opens the garage.
  - m14 (June 1985) walks his father around "the garage on Godfrey Avenue, the mill … the sign" before the book has rented it.
  - m13 (Aug 1987) comes before m14 (June 1985).
  - The contents show the overlap: Love & family runs 1974–2004, then Work runs 1983–1991.
  - Suggested order: m3 | m6, m4, m7 | m14, m13 | m8, m9 | m5, m11, m12. That makes chapter 5 "When everything changed · 1986–1991" with the sixty-percent customer leading straight into payroll, and puts the father before the lake.
- **m11, "At the end of second shift they called me to the back."** Second shift ends near midnight. On his last day, after signing payroll in the morning, a machinist would say "end of first shift" or "end of the day".
- **m5, Hector: "who was going to tell the twelve who stayed in 1991."** In 2019 at most five or six of them were left (m9: "Five of the twelve were still there in 2022"). "The ones who stayed in 1991" fixes it.
- **m13 vs m5, Tomás and the lathe.** "That's how I taught him the lathe ten years later" puts it in 1997, when Tomás was 17. m5 says he'd "been on the floor since he was sixteen" (1996). "When he was sixteen" fixes both.
- **Last page: "Every conversation in this book is kept in Raymond's own voice."** The book also holds Elena, Marisol, Tomás, Hector and Maya, and m5 is a Christmas-table conversation. Say "Every conversation in this book is kept, in the family's archive".
- **Soft, voice.** He says his father spoke "in Spanish and in English, depending on who was listening", and his father-in-law toasted "in Spanish". Yet no Spanish word appears anywhere in Ray's own voice. A 74-year-old from South Flores Street would keep at least the father's one line in the original. That's optional and only if it's true to the recording.
- **Soft.** The dedication and the people list both say "kept the books and kept me".
- **Soft.** Photo 03 caption, "Everything we owned still fit in that wagon." Seven years married with two kids (Tomás, 2, isn't in the frame) is a stretch, but it's a joke a man makes. Leave it unless a reader trips.
