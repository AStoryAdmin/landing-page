# Mission

This is the source of truth for what A Story is and how it sounds. It is the
company's own language, not a draft to be improved.

Nothing on the site should contradict it. Where the site needs a sentence, the
sentence should sound like these.

---

Most of a life goes undocumented. Not the big milestones — those get photographed. It's
the everyday: the story told at dinner and never written down, the reason everyone was
laughing in that photo, the small ordinary day that turns out to matter later. Those
slip away quietly. A Story exists to catch them, so the moments that make up a life are
still here for the people who'll want them.

Most tools for this look backward. They help you preserve a life that's already been
lived — a memoir to finish before it's too late. A Story works differently. It captures
the life you're living now and the one behind you, and it never assumes the story is
finished, because it isn't. Today is part of it too.

It works through conversation. A Story asks, listens, and remembers — following up the
way someone who knows you would, connecting what you share to the people, places, and
photos it belongs with, and laying it out on a timeline that keeps growing as your life
does.

And a life isn't a solo account. The same afternoon looks different to the person who
lived it, the child who was there, and the one who only heard about it for years. A
Story holds all of those voices on the same moment — not one narrator, but everyone who
was in the room. That's what makes it a documentary instead of a diary.

Any chapter can become something you hold — a beautifully made book, printed when
you're ready. But the story doesn't stop when the book is made. It keeps going, because
so do you.

---

## The argument inside it

Five movements. This is the **argument** the site has to carry — not a section list, not
a running order for the page. A designer may enter anywhere, compress two movements into
one composition, or hold one back. What they may not do is lose one, or break the logic
that connects them.

1. **The problem.** Most of a life goes undocumented. Not the milestones — those get
   photographed. The everyday slips away quietly.
2. **The difference.** Other tools look backward: a memoir to finish before it's too
   late. A Story takes the life being lived now as well, and never treats the story as
   finished.
3. **The mechanism.** It works through conversation. It asks, listens, remembers,
   follows up the way someone who knows you would, connects what you say to people,
   places and photographs, and lays it on a timeline that keeps growing.
4. **The distinctive claim.** A life isn't a solo account. The same afternoon looks
   different to everyone who was there, and A Story holds all those voices on one
   moment. *That is what makes it a documentary instead of a diary.*
5. **The object, and the open end.** Any chapter can become a printed book. The story
   doesn't stop when the book is made.

Movement 4 is the one no competitor can copy cheaply, and it is the one the previous
site buried. Movement 2 is what stops A Story reading as "an app for interviewing
grandparents."

## A warning from the last attempt

*"Most of a life goes undocumented"* was the homepage headline and the page title of the
earlier site. The 1,696-line specification instructed the builder not to preserve that
sequence, and it was removed. The current homepage contains no trace of the phrase.

Do not let that happen again. This language predates the design and outranks it.
